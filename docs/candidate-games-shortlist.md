# Candidate Games — Shortlist of 10
## For: Little Sylly Games — multiplayer-first expansion
## Status: Brainstorm shortlist for discussion

**Brief:** Light, theme-forward, quick, multiplayer-friendly. Popular/well-regarded source games. Nothing heavy.

**Filter applied:** Your existing catalogue already covers a lot of ground. Before proposing anything I checked it against what you have, to avoid suggesting a game you've effectively already built:

| You already have | So I'm avoiding |
|---|---|
| Secret Signals (Decrypto-like) | Decrypto, Codenames-style code interception |

That rules out most of the obvious BGG party-game top 10 (Codenames, Decrypto, Chameleon, Just One's clue mechanic). So this shortlist deliberately reaches for mechanics you *don't* yet have — wagering, spectrum-guessing, drawing, storytelling, real-time, and judging — each with a Little Sylly spin.

## 1. Wavelength → "Read the Room"
**Source:** Wavelength (spectrum guessing). BGG Golden Geek Best Party Game.
**Mechanic you don't have yet:** Analog spectrum estimation — a dial, not a word.
**The spin:** Pure social calibration. One player sees a hidden target between two opposites ("Overrated ↔ Underrated"), gives a clue, everyone dials to find it. Touchscreen dial is *better* than the cardboard original.
**Why it fits:** Fastest game in the box (60 sec/round), endless cheap content (word pairs), multiplayer-native (each device a private dial for the clue-giver). **Already brainstormed — concept B in the wavelength/wits doc.**
**Effort:** Low. **Multiplayer fit:** Excellent.

---

## 2. Wits & Wagers → "Going Once"
**Source:** Wits & Wagers (estimate + bet). The betting party game.
**Mechanic you don't have yet:** Wagering/betting on others' answers.
**The spin:** An auction house appraising absurd items. Everyone guesses a value, guesses line up, you bet chips on whose appraisal is closest *without going over*. Win by reading who knows, not by knowing.
**Why it fits:** The betting layer is a genuinely new mechanic for your box. Auction theme is warm and funny. **Already brainstormed — concept A in the wavelength/wits doc.**
**Effort:** Medium (needs a curated item-value content pool). **Multiplayer fit:** Excellent.

---

## 3. Telestrations → "Broken Telephone"
**Source:** Telestrations (draw → guess → draw, the "telephone" of doodles). Consistently top-rated laugh-out-loud party game.
**Mechanic you don't have yet:** Drawing on a touchscreen + a passing chain.
**The spin:** A whispered message degrades through a chain of bad drawings and worse guesses. Each player draws the previous player's guess, then the next guesses the drawing, all on their own device. The reveal of the full mangled chain is the payoff. Theme it as a chaotic group-chat game of "telephone" — or a courtroom sketch artist relaying testimony.
**Why it fits:** This is the single best multiplayer-first opportunity in the list — it *requires* individual devices to work well, and your MFS makes it possible. No game in your box uses drawing. Universally funny, no skill needed (bad art is the point).
**Effort:** Medium-high (needs a canvas drawing component — new territory). **Multiplayer fit:** Multiplayer-only, really.

---

## 4. Dixit → "Read Between the Lines"
**Source:** Dixit (evocative image + cryptic clue + vote). Spiel des Jahres winner, beloved for its art.
**Mechanic you don't have yet:** Abstract/evocative interpretation and voting.
**The spin:** A storyteller gives a poetic, vague clue about a surreal image. Everyone submits one of their own images that could fit the clue. All images are shuffled and shown; everyone votes which was the storyteller's. The trick: the clue must be *just* right — too obvious and everyone guesses it (storyteller scores nothing); too obscure and no one does (also nothing). The sweet spot is genius.
**Why it fits:** A more poetic, artful register than the rest of your catalogue — would broaden the box's emotional range. Pairs beautifully with a generated/curated art set.
**Effort:** High (needs a large pool of surreal images — content-heavy, and image generation/licensing is the hard part). **Multiplayer fit:** Excellent. **Flag:** the image content is the real cost and risk here.

---

## 5. Two Rooms and a Boom / Hidden-vote games → "House Party"
**Source:** Social hidden-role games with a *spatial/swap* twist.
**Mechanic you don't have yet:** Hidden roles with a trading/swap layer rather than missions.
**The spin:** A house party where guests secretly hold "vibes" cards (you're the life of the party, you're the buzzkill, you're secretly in love with the host). Players privately swap or reveal cards in timed rounds to achieve secret goals. Lighter and sillier than Bailed — more chaos, less logic.
**Why it fits:** Different flavour of social deduction from your Werewolf-adjacent Late to the Party — this one is about *trading and revealing* rather than *accusing*. Multiplayer makes the private swaps clean.
**Effort:** Medium. **Multiplayer fit:** Excellent. **Flag:** needs careful design to stay distinct from Late to the Party and Bailed — you'd have three social-deduction games. Maybe one too many.

---

## 6. Monikers / Celebrity → "Three Ways"
**Source:** Monikers (the three-round escalating describe game). A perennial top-tier party game.
**Mechanic you don't have yet:** The escalating three-round structure (describe freely → one word → charades) over a *shared, memorised* card set.
**The spin:** Same cards, three rounds, each more constrained. Round 1: describe with any words. Round 2: one word only. Round 3: act it out (or, for a phone game, a single sound/emoji). The comedy comes from everyone having to remember the same cards across rounds. Theme it as a group trying to explain increasingly weird concepts to each other.
**Why it fits:** The escalation structure is genuinely distinct from Like I'm Five's single-round restricted clue. Charades round is the challenge on a phone — could become "describe with only emoji" instead.
**Effort:** Medium. **Multiplayer fit:** Good (team-based). **Flag:** overlaps tonally with Like I'm Five; the three-round structure is what justifies it.

---

## 7. The Mind → "In Sync"
**Source:** The Mind (wordless cooperative number-sequencing). Spiel des Jahres nominee, a cult favourite.
**Mechanic you don't have yet:** Real-time cooperative tension with no communication.
**The spin:** The whole group must play their secret numbers in ascending order — but *no one can talk*. You hold cards (numbers), and you must sense when it's your turn to play based purely on timing and group intuition. A held breath of a game. Theme it as a group trying to stay perfectly in sync — finishing each other's sentences, no words allowed.
**Why it fits:** It's a *cooperative* game — your catalogue is almost entirely competitive. Adds a totally different emotional texture (shared tension, collective triumph). Extremely simple. Multiplayer-native (each device holds your hidden numbers, real-time sync via MFS).
**Effort:** Low-medium (the real-time sync is the technical interest). **Multiplayer fit:** Multiplayer-only, really. **This is my dark-horse pick** — it's unlike anything you have.

## 8. Herd Mentality → "Great Minds Think Alike... Or Do They"
**Source:** Herd Mentality (answer to match the majority). A breakout commercial hit, dead simple.
**Mechanic you don't have yet:** Majority-matching (score by being *average*, punished for being unique).
**The spin:** A question with many possible answers ("Name a breakfast food"). You score if your answer matches the *majority* of the group. The one odd-one-out each round gets a penalty. It's the inverse of trivia — there's no right answer, only the popular one.
**Why it fits:** Hmm — this is genuinely close to your Great Minds (think-alike matching) and You Get It? (group consensus). **This is the weakest fit on the list precisely because you've covered this space twice.** Included for completeness but I'd veto it myself.
**Effort:** Low. **Multiplayer fit:** Excellent. **Flag:** redundant with Great Minds. Likely cut.

---

## 9. Skull → "Poker Face"
**Source:** Skull (bluffing + bidding with hidden discs). A pure, elegant bluffing game.
**Mechanic you don't have yet:** Bluffing-and-bidding with a press-your-luck reveal.
**The spin:** Each player secretly places face-down cards (safe "flowers" or one deadly "skull"). Then players bid on how many cards they can flip without hitting a skull. Win two challenges and you win. Pure nerve and reading people — almost no luck. Theme it lightly: laying down "good vibes" cards with one "drama" card hidden, betting on how far the group's good mood goes before someone ruins it.
**Why it fits:** A bluffing/bidding game is a mechanic you don't have. Tiny ruleset, intense psychology, very short rounds. Works on individual devices for the hidden placement.
**Effort:** Low-medium. **Multiplayer fit:** Excellent. **Flag:** the bidding tension is subtle — needs good UX to land on a phone, and the theme needs work to feel Sylly rather than grim.

---

## 10. Fibbage / balderdash → "Bluff the Group"
**Source:** Fibbage (Jackbox) / Balderdash (invent a fake answer, fool the group).
**Mechanic you don't have yet:** Inventing plausible lies + spotting the truth among fakes.
**The spin:** A bizarre-but-true fact with a blank ("The official state ___ of Texas is the Dutch oven"). Everyone invents a fake answer to fill the blank. All fakes + the real answer are shuffled; everyone picks the one they think is true. Score for picking the truth AND for fooling others with your fake. Theme it as a group of unreliable friends each telling tall tales.
**Why it fits:** The "invent a convincing lie" mechanic is distinct from everything you have — it's generative, not just selective. Hugely funny. Naturally multiplayer (private answer submission, simultaneous reveal). Closest sibling is You Get It?'s ringer mechanic, but here *players* generate the fakes rather than the content file — a meaningful difference.
**Effort:** Medium (needs a content pool of weird-true facts). **Multiplayer fit:** Excellent. **This is my top pick alongside Telestrations** — it adds a genuinely new creative act (writing a lie) to the box.

# My honest recommendation

If the goal is to **showcase multiplayer** and add genuinely new mechanics rather than variations on what you have, the three that stand out:

**Broken Telephone (Telestrations)** — the purest "this only works because we now have individual devices" game. Drawing is a whole new interaction for your box, and the degrading-chain reveal is reliably the funniest thing at any party. It's the game that would make people say "oh, *this* is why they did multiplayer."

**Bluff the Group (Fibbage)** — adds *writing a convincing lie* as a creative act, which nothing in your catalogue does. Generative rather than selective. Endlessly replayable with a good fact pool.

**In Sync (The Mind)** — the dark horse. Your entire catalogue is competitive; this is a wordless cooperative breath-holder. It would diversify the *feeling* of the box, not just the mechanics. Technically the most interesting (real-time sync) and the simplest to explain.

The ones I'd deprioritise: Herd Mentality (you've covered it twice), Three Ways (overlaps Like I'm Five), and House Party (would give you a third social-deduction game alongside Late to the Party and Bailed).
