Hand Size: The Sandbox Steel-man Let's break down the friction point of hand sizes on a mobile browser screen. Hand Size of 3: Pros: Cleanest UI layout for portrait mobile screens. Extremely fast-paced. Cons: High RNG [random number generation] dependency. You can't realistically "hoard" a strategic combo because one bad draw forces your hand. Hand Size of 5: Pros: Deep strategic layer. You can hold onto a defensive card (like a Subtractor) while holding a lethal bomb (like the 99-setter) for the perfect moment. Cons: Clutters a mobile viewport. If text on custom cards is detailed, 5 cards side-by-side gets squished. The Designer's Pitch — Hand Size of 4: Let's aim for 4 cards. It gives you enough room to hold one high-value custom card, one defensive card, and two standard number cards, without completely ruining the horizontal screen estate on a phone. Deck Composition & Intermediate Numbers Honest take on the number cards: we should absolutely drop the intermediate filler numbers like +3, +4, +6, +7, +8, and +9. In a traditional card game, those numbers exist because you're using a standard poker deck. For our custom shp deck, intermediate numbers just muddy the mental math and slow down the pace. By keeping standard additions strictly to +1, +2, +5, and +10, the math becomes incredibly snappy and predictable, allowing players to focus entirely on card synergy and tactical plays rather than basic arithmetic. Smoothing Out the Early Game Slog Here is the friction point with traditional 99: the march from 0 to 60 carries zero tension because it's impossible to bust. It feels like administrative work before the actual game begins. Aside from your "Fast Start" (Start at 50) setting toggle, here are two mechanical ways we can smooth out and elevate the early game experience: The "Dream Acceleration" Rule: Any standard number card played while the running total is below 50 has its value doubled (+5 becomes +10, +10 becomes +20). This rockets the lobby into the danger zone within the first round of turns. Early Hand Building: Since there's no threat of busting under 50, players can use these early turns to "craft" their perfect endgame hand. We could allow players to decline playing a card and instead"Discard & Redraw" once per round if the count is low. PWA UI & Animation Feasibility Good news here: a sheep jumping over a fence animation is not too costly for a mobile browser PWA, provided we build it smart. We absolutely want to avoid heavy 3D rendering engines or massive sprite sheets. Instead, we can achieve a gorgeous, lightweight effect using simple CSS Keyframe Animations applied to a single SVG vector sheep asset. When a player submits a card, the engine fires a trigger that makes a tiny, styled SVG sheep translate along a smooth cubic-bezier curve over a central digital fence. It is incredibly cheap on processor performance, keeping the app lightweight and fast to load.
