## 3. The Core Loop

**In one sentence:** On your Command you do exactly one thing: open a door, march a piece one square, strike with a piece, or (Samurai only) Charge.

**The central tension:** every door is a gamble. You might find one of your own pieces, hand your opponent one of theirs, or find a fox, who sides with *you*. You're also hunting for their Shogun while hoping they don't open the door beside yours.

**Closest type:** ☑ Two-player duel or board/grid game

**One Battle, step by step:**
1. **The Battle intro** (§0c) says who opens the first door. In Battle 1 the Storyteller's fan toss decides; after that the first move **alternates every Battle, replays included** (§5).
2. **The first Command is always opening a door,** because nothing else exists yet.
3. **Raising the Banners:** the first *clan* piece anyone finds (anything but a fox) decides the colours. Whoever opened that door takes that piece's clan, and the other player takes the other. If a fox turns up before then, she sides with the player who found her and takes that player's colour once the banners are raised.
4. **Players alternate Commands.** Each Command is one action from the table below; what may strike what is in §4.
5. **The Battle ends** when a Shogun is struck, when the last lantern goes out, or when a player is Cornered (§5). The winner earns an Honour Seal (§6).

| Action | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Open** (a door) | Slide one closed door open; the piece behind it stays where it is. Anyone may open any door, even one hiding an enemy. Opening a fox brings her to your side (§4c). |
| **March** | Move one of your pieces one square up, down, left or right into an empty square. |
| **Strike** | Capture an enemy piece that is in the open, following §4. The striker moves into the captured piece's square, except the Archer, who shoots without moving. |
| **Charge** | Samurai only: march exactly two squares in a straight line, through an empty square into an empty square. A Charge can't strike. |

Pieces behind closed doors can't be struck, and nobody owns a piece until its door is opened. A Command that is neither an Open nor a Strike is **quiet**; the Last Lantern (§5) counts quiet Commands.

**Simultaneous or sequential?** Strictly sequential: one Command at a time.

**How the phone moves:** one phone each, or one phone flat on the table between the two players. Nothing needs hiding, so there are no hand-over screens.

## 4. Rule Interactions

This section is the single source of truth for the rules; every other section points back to it.

### 4a. The household (per clan: 13 pieces, plus 4 foxes shared; 30 doors)

| Piece | Rank | Count | Moves | Strikes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Shogun** | 8 | 1 | 1 square, orthogonally | Up close (an orthogonal neighbour) |
| **Guard** | 7 | 2 | 1 square, orthogonally | Up close |
| **Monk** | 6 | 2 | 1 square, orthogonally | **Diagonally only:** strikes a piece on one of the four diagonal squares by moving into it; can't strike orthogonally |
| **Samurai** | 5 | 2 | 1 square, or **Charge** 2 | Up close |
| **Ninja** | 4 | 1 | 1 square, orthogonally | **Leap only:** jumps over exactly one piece in a straight line and strikes the first piece beyond it, at any distance; lands on the struck piece's square; can't strike an adjacent piece. A Leap is only ever a strike, never a move. |
| **Archer** | 3 | 2 | 1 square, orthogonally | **Volley:** shoots without moving at a piece exactly 2 or 3 squares away in a straight line, over whatever is in between; can't hit an adjacent piece |
| **Farmer** | 2 | 3 | 1 square, orthogonally | Up close: another Farmer, an Archer, a fox, or an Unguarded Shogun |
| **Fox** (sides with whoever finds her) | — (off the ladder) | 4 in total, shared | 1 square, orthogonally | Up close: the enemy Shogun only, even when he is Guarded |

**Counts.** 1 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 2 + 3 = 13 per clan, 26 for both, plus 4 foxes = 30 doors. With **A Pair** of foxes (§7), each clan gets 4 Farmers instead of 3 (28 + 2 = 30).

The ladder runs Shogun, Guard, Monk, Samurai, Ninja, Archer, Farmer.

### 4b. Who can strike whom

Attacker down the side, target across the top, both in rank order. Equal ranks may strike each other unless stated. The Monk, the Ninja and the Archer ignore rank.

| Attacker ↓ / Target → | Shogun | Guard | Monk | Samurai | Ninja | Archer | Farmer | Fox |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Shogun** | ✓* | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ |
| **Guard** | ✓* | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ |
| **Monk** (diagonal) | ✓* | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ |
| **Samurai** | ✓* | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ |
| **Ninja** (Leap) | ✓* | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Archer** (Volley) | ✓* | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ |
| **Farmer** | ✓* | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Fox** | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ |

\* Only when that Shogun is **Unguarded** (4c). Reading the Shogun column: a fox can always strike him; anyone else only once his Guard is gone. Reading the Fox column: only a **Farmer** or the **Ninja** can strike a fox. The Monk and Archer rows are identical (everything but a fox or a Guarded Shogun); they differ only in *how* they reach it. The Ninja's row is the same plus foxes.

### 4c. Special states and rulings

1. **Guarded / Unguarded.** A Shogun is **Guarded** while at least one Guard of his clan is still in the castle, **including a Guard still behind a closed door**. While Guarded, only a fox can strike him. When both his Guards have fallen he is **Unguarded**, and any piece that can reach him with its own strike pattern may strike him, whatever its rank.
2. **The foxes.** There are four foxes behind the doors (two with A Pair). They belong to neither clan at the start.
   - **Finding a fox:** whoever opens her door brings her to their side. She takes that player's colour and stays where she was found.
   - **Each clan's share:** a clan may hold at most half the foxes. If the finder already holds their share, the fox sides with the **other** clan instead, wherever she stands. (With 4 foxes: find two and they're yours; find a third and she goes to your opponent.) Each clan's fox slots are shown on screen, so this is never a surprise.
   - **Before the banners:** a fox never decides the colours. Found before the banners are raised, she waits, sides with her finder (or the other clan, under the share rule), and takes their colour once the banners rise.
   - **Strikes:** a fox strikes only the enemy Shogun, even when he is Guarded. She can be struck only by a **Farmer** or the **Ninja**.
   - A fox still behind a closed door when the Battle ends belongs to nobody.
3. **Strikes replace.** The striker moves into the captured piece's square. The Archer stays where he is.
4. **Rank for up-close strikes** (Shogun, Guard, Samurai, Farmer): equal or lower rank only, subject to the table. The Farmers are the exception in the other direction. They are the lowest rank but may strike a fox (farmers have always chased foxes off), an Archer (a crowd of farmers can overrun an archer) and each other.
5. **The Ninja's Leap** exists only when exactly one piece (the "screen": either clan, opened or still behind a door) stands between her and the first piece beyond it, that piece is an enemy in the open she may strike, and she isn't adjacent to it. Any number of empty squares before and after the screen is fine. If any of those conditions fails, there is simply no Leap in that direction.
6. **The Archer** needs an enemy in the open exactly 2 or 3 squares away in a straight line. What stands in the squares between (nothing, a friend, an enemy, a closed door) doesn't matter: the arrow goes over. He can't hit a fox or a Guarded Shogun. Closed doors in his range are fishing spots: an enemy found there can be shot on the next Command.
7. **The Samurai's Charge** moves exactly two squares in a straight line; both squares must be empty; it can't strike; there's no cooldown.
8. **The Monk** marches like everyone else but strikes only diagonally, so she can't strike an orthogonal neighbour, and orthogonal neighbours may strike her. Blocked orthogonal squares never stop a diagonal strike.

### 4d. Checks

- **Closed loop (three-way):** Fox → Shogun → Ninja → Fox. The fox beats the Shogun, the Shogun beats the Ninja, the Ninja beats the fox. This is deliberate and is the heart of the game. The Farmers sit on the same kind of loop from below: Farmer → Fox → Shogun → Farmer.
- **Distance pairs** (mutual strikes decided by range): Archer ⇄ Farmer, Samurai, Guard, Ninja, Monk. Up close the other piece wins; at 2 or 3 squares the Archer wins, so whoever strikes first wins the exchange. The Samurai's Charge exists to cross the Archer's range. The Monk has a diagonal version: she can hit most pieces from a corner they can't hit back from.
- **Dead ends:**
  - The fox beats exactly one piece (the Shogun) and is beaten by exactly two (the Farmer and the Ninja). Balanced by being rare and neutral until found.
  - A Guarded Shogun can't be touched except by a fox. Balanced by the Guards being strikeable (by a Shogun, Guard, Monk, Ninja or Archer) and by the Last Lantern.
  - The Guard and the Monk can't be struck by a Samurai or a Farmer.
- **Cross-group links:** none (one board).
- **Wildcards:** none beyond the fox. She ignores the Guard, and can't be targeted by anyone but the Farmer and the Ninja.
- **Order of resolution:** a Command resolves its one action, then the game checks, in order: a Shogun captured (win) → the Last Lantern count → whether the next player is Cornered.
- **Temporary changes:** none. Sylly Mode isn't designed yet (§8).

## 5. Winning

| Field | Your answer |
|---|---|
| **How a Battle ends** | (1) **A Shogun is struck**: the striker's player wins ("Found you!"). (2) **The last lantern goes out**: after 12 quiet Commands in a row (6 each) with no door opened and no strike by either player, the Battle ends and the player with **more pieces in the open** wins. (3) **Cornered**: a player with no legal Command on their turn (no closed door to open and no legal March, Strike or Charge) loses. |
| **How the Campaign ends** | One Honour Seal per Battle won; **first to 3 Seals** wins the Campaign (setting: first to 1, 3 or 5). |
| **Ties** | Only at the Last Lantern: equal counts of pieces in the open is a **Truce**. A Truce earns no Seal and the Battle is replayed. |
| **Series structure** | The Storyteller's fan toss picks who goes first in Battle 1. After that the first move **alternates every Battle, replays included**. A replay keeps the Battle number ("Battle 2, again"). |
| **Length** | One Battle: median about 50 Commands `[SIM]`, about 100–160 seconds `[PLAYTEST: 6 Battles]`. First-to-3 Campaign: 3–5 Battles, roughly 5–12 minutes `[ASSUMPTION]`. |
| **Stall guardrails** | The Last Lantern (closing clock, both players) and Cornered (no legal move loses). |

**The Last Lantern counts pieces in the open only.** A closed door counts for nobody, even though both players could work out what's left. That rewards opening doors when you know you're ahead. A fox who has been found counts for her side; a fox still behind a door counts for nobody. Any Open or Strike by either player relights all the lanterns (the count resets to 0).

## 6. Scoring

| What happened | Who scores | How much | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Struck the enemy Shogun | The striker's player | 1 Honour Seal | Ends the Battle at once |
| More pieces in the open when the last lantern goes out | That player | 1 Honour Seal | |
| Opponent Cornered | The other player | 1 Honour Seal | Rare |
| Truce (equal count at the Last Lantern) | Nobody | 0 | The Battle is replayed; first move flips |
| Leaving the Field (quitting) | The other player | Wins the whole Campaign | |

**Balance:** the player who moves first wins 49–53% of bot Battles, which is noise `[SIM]`; alternating the first move is a courtesy, not a correction. No negative points; Seals only go up.

**Every way to act:**
1. **Tap-tap (primary):** tap one of your pieces; it lifts and glows, and its legal squares light up with shape-coded marks (on the boards: a glowing lamp-amber dot for March, amber double chevrons for Charge, ink corner brackets around a strike target; still to draw at build: an arc for a Leap with the screen piece outlined, and the Monk's diagonal mark). Tap a lit square to act. The message card names the piece and its options.
2. **Drag and drop:** drag your piece onto a lit square.
3. **Open a door: two taps, no popup.** The first tap lifts the door, a vague shadow shows through the paper, and an **"Open?"** button in the brand colour appears on it; the message card reads *"Somebody's there…"*. A second tap on the same door opens it; tapping anywhere else cancels. This replaces v4's confirmation box, which interrupted about half of all Commands. The shadow is the same for every door: it never hints at what is behind it.
4. **Long-press any piece** (yours, theirs or a fox) to jump to its row in How to Play's gallery.

**Feedback:** only legal squares light up; tapping an unlit square just deselects. The last Command is marked on the board with a dashed lamp-amber outline, and the message card says what it was ("Priya's Monk just marched"), so the waiting player can see what happened.

**Undo:** none. A Command is final once made.

## 18. How to Play — Teaching Points

1. **The doors:** every piece starts behind a closed door. Anyone may open any door.
2. **Your Command:** one action: open a door, march, or strike.
3. **Raising the Banners:** the first clan piece found decides who is Crimson and who is Indigo.
4. **The ladder:** higher strikes lower, up close.
5. **The specialists:** Archer (2 or 3 squares), Ninja (Leap over one piece), Monk (diagonal), Samurai (Charge 2), Farmer (lowest, but chases off foxes). *Easy to miss: the Archer and the Ninja can't hit an adjacent piece.*
6. **The Guard:** while a Guard stands, even one still behind a door, the Shogun is Guarded; when both fall he is Unguarded.
7. **The foxes:** find one and she sides with you, up to your share. She's the only piece that can reach a Guarded Shogun, and only a Farmer or the Ninja can stop her. *Easy to miss: your third fox goes to your opponent.*
8. **The Last Lantern:** 12 quiet Commands in a row and the Battle ends; most pieces **in the open** wins. *Easy to miss: closed doors don't count.*
9. **Winning and Scoring:** strike their Shogun, or lead when the last lantern goes out; one Seal per Battle; first to 3; a Truce is replayed; the first move alternates.
10. **✨ Sylly Mode:** not yet.

## 19. Open Questions & Design Notes

### Decisions log

**v5 (11 Nov 2026, Opus review with the owner):**
- **World:** the Paper Castle (hide-and-seek in a castle of paper doors). Tone: **war with a wink**, a real war between clans played like a children's game. Narrator: **the Storyteller** (a rakugo teller, named only in his role line).
- **The Geisha became the Fox.** The mechanic is unchanged, but the suite's audience includes children and the app is named for one; "Geisha for hire", "The Floating World" and "Ronin hunt Geisha" read badly there. The fox spirit is folklore, kid-safe, and naturally sides with whoever finds her. The setting became The Foxes: A Pair / A Skulk.
- **Plain-English cast:** Daimyo → Guard, Onmyoji → Monk, Kunoichi → Ninja, Gunner → Archer, Ronin → Farmer. Chosen for a child reading a tile. Two changes also fit the rules: the Archer's arrow arcs *over* pieces, and farmers chasing foxes is instantly intuitive.
- **Shogun's Shame cut.** It was a rule players couldn't see: it fired in about 5% of bot Battles, needed its own meter, and changed how the Guard worked. The Last Lantern already stops stalling `[SIM]`. Its code stays in the reference engines, switched off, for comparison runs.
- **A Truce replay flips the first move**, so the rule is simply "the first move alternates every Battle, replays included". Who goes first doesn't change results `[SIM]`.
- **Unveil confirmation box removed** in favour of a two-tap door (§14).
- **Collision renames:** Vault → **Leap** (Flawless and Secret Signals both use "Vault"); Exposed → **Unguarded** (Flawless uses "Exposed"); Long Night → **The Last Lantern** (Counting Sheep's rounds are Nights); Surrounded → **Cornered**; Ivory → **Indigo** (readability on the paper board).
- **Vanguard, Rearguard, the Muster and The Omen retired:** the players' names, the Intro and the fan toss do those jobs.
- **Piece Card and Rule Reference folded into How to Play's gallery tab** (the suite's tap-hold standard).

**Stage 1b (11 Nov 2026):** three directions drawn (the Lamplit Room, the Picture Scroll, the Shadow Theatre); the owner took A's world with C's Castle, and **Lantern gold #B8862B** over castle pine, tatami olive and bronze, which "appear nowhere in the palette". Detail in §0d.

**v1–v4 (Oct 2026, with Sonnet):** two players only; capturing the Shogun is the win; the Guard (any Guard, opened or not); the neutral fox piece and the share rule; the Farmer (v4's Ronin) may strike a fox and an Archer; the Ninja's Leap takes anything but a Guarded Shogun, foxes included; the Archer's range is 2–3; the Monk ranks above the Samurai; the Last Lantern at 12 quiet Commands counting pieces in the open; Honour Seals and the Campaign.

**Review of the design against Banqi (v5):**
- **Banqi's weaknesses and how they were answered.** Wipe-out wins drag on and draw → **capture the Shogun instead** (the most valuable change). Six of seven pieces move the same → **distinct moves** (real tactics, taught by tapping). Early leads snowball → **foxes** let the trailing player come back. Flip risk and a slow opening are kept: weighing a flip is the genre's core skill, and Battles are short.
- **How the rules piled up.** Capture-the-Shogun made a random instant loss possible → the Guard → an untouchable Shogun → a piece that bypasses the Guard (then winning 80% of Battles) → Farmer and Ninja counters plus the share rule; meanwhile hiding plus the Guard made stalling worthwhile → a stall clock and the closing clock → draws → Truce and replay → counting only pieces in the open. Each step was justified by sim evidence (an earlier "expose both Shoguns" closing rule drew 12–15%), but the chain is why the rule count is high. v5 stops changing rules here and lets human playtests decide anything further.
- **The test for every rule:** a rule a player can discover by tapping is cheap; a rule they can't see is expensive. After v5, every rule has something on screen: the Guard ring, the fox slots, the lantern row.

**Deviations from strong defaults:** the Castle is a fixed-height board, not a scrolling Stack; it is one column, but the board must never scroll (the revamp's loosened layout rule, `docs/rules/ui-revamp.md` § 2). Stage 2 logs it.

### Balance and tuning

- **v5 defaults (A Skulk, no Shame):** about **69%** of bot Battles end with a Shogun struck, about **29%** at the Last Lantern, about 2% in a Truce `[SIM: round 6, "Shame off" row, aggro bots]`. With A Pair: about 53–58% / 38–42%, Truce about 4%, median 60–62 Commands `[SIM]`.
- **Fox share of Shogun strikes:** 71% with A Skulk, 49% with A Pair, with the Farmer and Ninja counters `[SIM]`; a fox dies about once per Battle (A Skulk) `[SIM]`. Owner playtest: the Geisha (now fox) made the winning strike in 4 of 6 Battles, under the v3 rules before the Farmer counter `[PLAYTEST]`.
- **Per piece, per Battle, both clans** (strikes / losses): Archer 1.8 / 2.1, Monk 1.5 / 1.7, Ninja 1.0 / 1.0, Farmer 1.8 / 2.6, Samurai 1.1 / 1.9, Guard 2.1 / 1.7, fox 0.5 / 1.0 `[SIM]`.
- **First-mover:** 49–53% `[SIM]`. First fox found: that clan wins about half `[SIM]`.
- Full tables: `docs/new-ideas/hide and shogun/balance-findings.md`.

### Risks to playtest, and things that might be hard to build

- **How much there is to learn.** Seven piece types, five strike patterns, about eight exceptions in the strike table. Tapping covers most of it; the Practice tab (§24) must carry the rest. This is the biggest risk for a mixed-age party audience.
- **Fox luck:** does a fox win feel like an exciting comeback or a random loss?
- **The evidence is thin:** almost all of it comes from heuristic bots tuned to stand-in measures; there is one 6-Battle human playtest.
- **Board size on small phones:** six rows of ~54 px tiles at 320×452 leave little room for the header and the lantern row.
- **Watching it:** a two-player duel gives the rest of the room less to do; the Storyteller's moments must carry it.

### Out of scope for v1

### Sample B: the gamble of opening a door

In Sample A: Command 2 (Priya finds a fox, which sides with whoever finds her, so finding one first is a prize); Command 3 (Mia opens a piece that helps only its own clan, a modest one); Command 4 (Priya opens an Indigo Shogun: it hands Indigo a powerful piece, but puts it next to her fox). Opening a door is symmetrical: nobody knows what is behind it, and the piece goes to its own clan, not to the player who opened the door. A fox is the exception: she sides with the finder.

### Sample C: mini-scenarios

Each starts mid-Battle; all other squares are empty unless stated.

**C1 — Charging into the Archer's blind spot.** Indigo Samurai at C6; Crimson Archer at C3; C4 and C5 empty. The Archer hits squares exactly 2 or 3 away in a line (C1, C5, C6, A3, E3…), so C6 is *in* range. The Samurai **Charges** C6 → C4, ending adjacent, where the Archer can't hit. Unless the Archer escapes, the Samurai strikes C3 next Command (rank 5 beats 3).

**C2 — The Leap saves the Shogun.** Indigo Shogun at B1; Crimson fox at B2 (adjacent: a threat Indigo must answer now); Indigo Farmer at B4; Indigo Ninja at B6; B3 and B5 empty. The **Ninja Leaps** from B6 over the Farmer at B4 (the one screen) and strikes the fox at B2, landing there. The Shogun lives. (Had the Ninja stood next to the fox, there would be no Leap. Had the piece beyond the screen been behind a closed door, there would be no Leap either.)

**C3 — The Guard falls.** Crimson Shogun at D1; Crimson's last Guard at B1 (the other has already fallen); Indigo Archer at B3; Indigo Farmer at D2, adjacent to the Shogun; B2 empty. The Farmer can't strike the Shogun yet: he's Guarded. So the **Archer Volleys** B1 from B3 (exactly 2 squares), taking the last Guard. The Crimson Shogun is now **Unguarded**, and the Farmer beside him threatens him next Command. Crimson must strike the Farmer (the Shogun outranks it) or run.

**C4 — The Monk strikes across a corner.** Indigo Monk at B4; Crimson Archer at C3 (diagonal); B3 and C4 are closed doors, so she's boxed in orthogonally. The **Monk strikes diagonally** into C3. She couldn't strike either orthogonal square even if they held enemies.

**C5 — The fox share (A Skulk).** Crimson already has two foxes. Crimson opens E5: a third fox. Crimson already holds its share, so she **sides with Indigo Moon** and stands at E5 for Indigo. If E5 is next to Crimson's Shogun, Indigo's new fox can strike him at once, Guarded or not. Late in a Battle, opening a door might hand your opponent their fox.

**C6 — A Farmer catches a fox.** Indigo Farmer at C3; a Crimson fox at C2, adjacent, and one step from Indigo's Shogun. The **Farmer strikes C2** and takes her: something no Samurai or Guard could do.

**C7 — The Last Lantern.** Twelve Commands in a row (six each) pass with no door opened and no strike. The last lantern goes out. Crimson has 9 pieces in the open and Indigo has 8 (closed doors count for nobody), so Crimson wins the Battle and the Seal. At 8 each it would be a Truce: no Seal, and the Battle is replayed.

**C8 — A Campaign, with a Truce.** Mia and Priya play first to 3. **Battle 1** (Mia first): Mia wins, 1–0. **Battle 2** (Priya first): a Truce, no Seal. **Battle 2, replayed** (the first move flips: Mia first): Priya wins, 1–1. **Battle 3** (Priya first): Mia wins, 2–1. **Battle 4** (Mia first): Mia wins, 3–1, and **Mia takes the Campaign**.

**Cornered** is rare (it needs no closed doors left and every piece boxed in); it is covered by the rules (§5) and the harness, not a sample.

### Sample D: a bot's Command

Priya plays against the bot on **Normal**. The bot's Ninja could Leap onto Priya's Archer, or the bot could open a door next to Priya's revealed Shogun. The bot weighs the open door by what could still be behind it, using only the count of pieces not yet seen (which a careful human could also work out), never the actual piece. With several foxes still unseen, there's a real chance of handing Priya one there, so it takes the sure Leap instead `[ILLUSTRATIVE]`. It never knows what is behind a door until one is opened.

## 23. Hints & Tips

**Beginner:**
- Open doors far from your known pieces early on; a door next to your revealed piece might hide something that strikes it `[ORIGINAL: Banqi]`.
- Keep a Farmer or the Ninja near your Shogun once foxes are about `[ASSUMPTION]`.
- Don't stand an important piece exactly 2 or 3 squares in line with an enemy Archer `[ASSUMPTION]`.

**Advanced:**
- Count what is still unseen: late in a Battle the odds behind a door are knowable `[ORIGINAL: Banqi]`.
- Closed doors in your Archer's range are fishing spots: open one, and an enemy found there can be shot next Command `[SIM: the fishing bot]`.
- When you are ahead in pieces in the open, slow down: the Last Lantern rewards the leader `[SIM: a quiet player keeps a small edge at 12]`.
- With A Skulk, think twice about opening a door once you hold two foxes: a third goes to your opponent `[ASSUMPTION]`.

**Surprises:** the fox decides most Battles with A Skulk `[SIM]`; Cornered almost never happens `[SIM]`.

**Candidate hints (not built):** a danger mark on your pieces an enemy can strike next Command (no hidden information involved).

**The Practice tab must teach:** the Guard (a closed Guard still counts), the fox share, and the Last Lantern counting only pieces in the open.

## 24. Practice Tab (Tutorial)

**Chosen form:** ☑ **Lessons**

- **Why:** the game has many distinct actions, each best learned on its own small board; a single scripted Battle would bury the specialists. Honeycomb Hills is the reference.
- **Lessons** (one goal and one success check each, all on small fixed boards):
  1. **Open a door:** open one; the banners rise. *Check:* a door opened.
  2. **The ladder:** strike a lower piece up close. *Check:* the strike lands.
  3. **The Archer:** Volley a piece 2 or 3 squares away. *Check:* the Volley lands.
  4. **The Ninja:** Leap over a screen. *Check:* the Leap lands.
  5. **The Monk:** strike diagonally. *Check:* the diagonal strike lands.
  6. **The Samurai:** Charge past an Archer. *Check:* ends adjacent to it.
  7. **The Guard:** take the last Guard, then the Shogun. *Check:* the Shogun falls.
  8. **The foxes:** find one, then stop the enemy's with a Farmer. *Check:* the enemy fox struck.
  9. **The Last Lantern:** watch a count, with a slideshow fallback. *Check:* reaches the count.
- **Hidden information:** each lesson board is fixed, so there's nothing to hide.
- **Sylly Mode:** not covered (deferred).
- **Length and exit:** about 30–60 seconds per lesson; leave at any point.
- **Not taught here:** the Campaign and the full strike chart (How to Play and the gallery cover those).
